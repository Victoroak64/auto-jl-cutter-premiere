J/L

Auto J/L Cutterv0.2

Adobe Premiere Pro extension · internal tool · test release

It takes the cuts in your talking footage (the jump cuts) and turns all of them into J-cuts and L-cuts at once. The audio clips alternate between two tracks, the lines of speech overlap by the number of frames you choose, and the lips stay in sync. What you would do clip by clip with the Rolling Edit tool, it does in one click.

This is a test release. Try it on a test project first, not on a job with a deadline. Report anything odd, even small things: there's a button that copies a ready-made diagnostic for that.

What J-cuts and L-cuts are

J-cut: you HEAR the next clip before you SEE it VIDEO [====== clip 1 ======][===== clip 2 =====] ← the video cut stays put A1 [====== line 1 ======] A2 [####===== line 2 =====] ← line 2 starts under line 1 L-cut: the previous clip keeps TALKING over the new image VIDEO [===== clip 1 =====][====== clip 2 ======] ← the new image arrives early A1 [====== line 1 ==####] ← line 1 finishes on top A2 [====== line 2 ======]

Because every audio clip stays attached to its own video, lips never drift out of sync. The price of laying one line over another is that the sequence gets shorter: N frames per cut.

Requirements

  • Premiere Pro 2025 or 2026 (tested on 26.5)
  • macOS or Windows: there's an installer for each
  • Any interface language. The extension finds the crossfade's name in your Premiere's language on its own.
  • Quit Premiere completely before installing

How to use

1Cut the talking first

The plugin works on the cuts. Have your jump cuts ready (pauses removed), with video on V1 and audio on the source track, usually A1, and an empty alternate track, usually A2. Use it at the start of the edit, before B-roll, music and captions.

2Scope: where to apply

Whole timeline uses every cut between pieces of the same file. Selection uses only the clips you selected, even from different files. The panel always shows the active sequence and how many clips are selected.

3Tracks

Source is where the audio is; Alternate is where every other clip goes. The first clip always stays on the source track. New tracks show up in the list on their own.

4Mode and Overlap

Pick the mode (table below) and how many frames the lines overlap. 4 frames is a good start. Milliseconds also work, converted with the sequence's real frame rate.

5Analyze, then Apply

Analyze shows what will happen without changing anything: how many cuts, how many clips change track and how much shorter the sequence gets, plus any warnings. Apply does it, verifies every step, and the final report tells you what was done.

The controls

ControlWhat it does
InterleaveOnly alternates the audio clips between the two tracks. No overlap, nothing gets shorter.
J-cutAt every cut, the next line starts under the previous one.
L-cutAt every cut, the previous line finishes over the new image.
J + L and RhythmAlternates J and L. Rhythm 1 = J, L, J, L… · Rhythm 2 = J, J, L, L… If the rhythm is larger than the number of cuts, Analyze tells you.
Overlap + UnitHow many frames (or milliseconds) the lines overlap. The sequence gets that much shorter per cut.
CrossfadeA light fade at the start and end of each audio clip, so the handover between lines is smooth. 3 frames is usually enough.
Backup sequenceDuplicates the sequence before changing it. It shows up in the project panel with the date and time in its name, and you stay on the original.
UndoAlways visible; lights up when there's something to undo on the open timeline. In this version it works for Interleave; after J/L use Cmd+Z / Ctrl+Z or the backup.

Warnings you may see

  • "No cuts found": the timeline has no cuts. Cut the talking first.
  • "A2 already has clips": the alternate track already has audio in the way. The button turns into Apply anyway and only applies on the second click. If your camera audio uses two tracks (A1 + A2), pick another empty track as Alternate.
  • "…will be shortened too": clips on other tracks cross the cuts. They get shorter too, so nothing drifts out of sync.
  • "Cut skipped": a piece is too short to lose N frames, so that cut is left as it is.

Reporting a problem

Open Log (bottom of the panel) and click Copy diagnostics. It copies your Premiere version, OS, language, extension version and the full log. Paste it along with:

  • what you were doing (mode, frames, scope)
  • a screenshot of the timeline zoomed in on the cut, if it's visual

Known limitations

  • Undo only exists for Interleave in this version. For J/L, use Cmd+Z / Ctrl+Z or turn on Backup first.
  • Undo lives in the panel: closing the panel or Premiere loses it (Premiere's own Cmd+Z still works).
  • J/L makes the sequence shorter on purpose. That's what lets the lines overlap with lips in sync.
